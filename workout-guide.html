Between sets

Rest, then a two-second check-in

After a set the rest timer starts (1:00 to 3:00, or off), with +30s and Skip. A small bar asks how the set felt. Ignore it and it goes away on its own.

Each set keeps its rating, and the day's effort for that exercise is their average. Two easy sessions in a row can bring a step up forward.

Getting stronger

It tells you when to go up, and when to back off

Each exercise has its own step (say +5 lb) and timing (say every 2 weeks). Going up never happens on its own: you get an ↑ on the circle and a prompt in the hold sheet.

Step up

Due when the time is up and your last two sessions at these numbers had every set done.

Sooner, if it's easy

Two full sessions in a row rated Easy bring it forward, once you've had a week at the current numbers.

Back off

Two of your last three sessions stopped short? The hold sheet offers to step back down.

Planning

A weekly plan, and highlights for today

Set which muscle groups you train on which days. Each day, the exercises in those groups that you've gone longest without are highlighted in red, and turn green as you finish them.

No plan? It learns your usual week from what you log, and tells you when your logs and your plan disagree. Day templates (Leg day A, Push day) can fill a day with exact exercises.
